# DisplayPort over the Dragon Q8B's USB-C ports

Both connectors are outputs of `qcom_display` now: connector 0 is DP-1 (output 1: VIG0, LM_0, CTL_0, INTF_0), connector 1 is DP-2 (output 2: VIG1, LM_1, CTL_1, INTF_4), each hot plugged on its port's reports. With both forced on (`usbc_dp0 forced`, `usbc_dp1 forced`) app_server shows three displays (5760x1080) and both paths run without underruns; a simulated report on port 1 (`simulate_dp_report 1`) adds DP-2 and takes it away again.

What a second (and third) display needs, what is already proven on the board without a monitor, and what is left. The HDMI output (DP2 through the CH7218A bridge) is the firmware's trained link and does not need any of this.

## The chain, per USB-C connector

| | Connector 0 | Connector 1 |
|---|---|---|
| DisplayPort controller (MDSS 0) | DP0, 0xae90000 | DP1, 0xae98000 |
| Combo PHY (QMP USB43DP) | 0x88eb000 | 0x8903000 |
| SBU mux (PI3USB102, `gpio-sbu-mux`) | enable GPIO 101 (active low), select GPIO 164 | enable GPIO 48 (active low), select GPIO 47 |
| Port reports | pmic_glink alternate mode, port 0 | port 1 |

1. **The port report** (`qcom_adsp`, owner USBC_PAN, `USBC_NOTIFY_IND`): SVID 0xff01 means DisplayPort; the pin assignment (C, E: four DP lanes; D: two DP lanes and USB 3), the orientation, HPD and the HPD IRQ. Each report is acknowledged (`ALTMODE_PAN_ACK`). Done; no report has arrived yet, because nothing was plugged in.
2. **The SBU mux**: enable, and select = reverse orientation; off when the port is not in DisplayPort mode (Linux' `gpio-sbu-mux.c`). Proven to switch (the GPIOs).
3. **The combo PHY** (Linux' `phy-qcom-qmp-combo.c`, `sc8280xp_usb43dpphy_cfg`, offsets v5: com 0x0000, txa 0x0400, txb 0x0a00, dp_serdes 0x2000, dp_dp_phy 0x2200): USB30_PRIM_GDSC, the aux/ref/com aux clocks, L9D and L4D, its resets; the common block in USB3+DP mode with the orientation in TYPEC_CTRL; the AUX block (`qmp_v4_dp_aux_init`); the DP serdes at the link rate, both TX blocks, `qmp_v456_configure_dp_phy` (lane power from the lane count and orientation), and the swing/pre-emphasis tables during training. Proven on both connectors: the PLL locks at RBR and the PHY reports ready (`qcom_probe`'s `dp_phy_test()`; connector 1's PHY: USB30_SEC_GDSC 0x10004, clocks 0x10054/0x8c010/0x10058, resets 0x5000c/0xb9020, L4B and L3B). Its registers read back as their byte in all four lanes.
4. **dispcc 0**: DP0's link RCG (0x21a4) from the PHY's link clock (source 1), its pixel RCG (0x21d8) from the PHY's VCO divider clock (source 2) with M/N for the mode; the AUX RCG (0x2238) from XO; branches aux 0x206c, link 0x205c, link intf 0x2060, pixel 0x2070. DP1: link RCG 0x2268 from its PHY (source 2), AUX RCG 0x22d0, branches aux 0x20a0, link 0x2084. Proven on both: the link branch runs from the PHY, the AUX branch from XO.
5. **The controller's AUX** (`msm_dp_aux`): reset, timeout count and limits 0xffff, enable; then the transfers `qcom_display`'s `DisplayPort` already does. Proven on both: a native DPCD read with nothing plugged in ends in an AUX timeout after 55 ms, as it should.
6. **Link training** (Linux' `dp_ctrl.c`), written (`UsbcDisplayPort::_Train()`): DPCD caps, the sink's highest rate first (HBR3 only with TPS3), lower ones when it does not train, with its lane count; TPS1 with the swing/pre-emphasis the sink asks for (the PHY's v5 tables for RBR/HBR and for HBR2/HBR3) until clock recovery, TPS2/3 until channel equalization and interlane alignment. The PHY's PLL locks at every rate (RBR, HBR, HBR2, HBR3: its serdes tables and VCO divider per rate, checked when no sink answers). Proven without a sink: the DPCD read fails cleanly and nothing is left half set; the controller confirms sending training patterns 1 to 3 (MAINLINK_READY bits 3 to 5). The training itself against a sink is untested.
7. **The stream**: the pixel clock from the PHY's VCO divider (810 MHz at RBR; DP0 pixel RCG 0x21d8 source 2, DP1 0x2250 source 4; branches pixel 0x2070/0x2078, link intf 0x2060/0x2088), the timing engine (DP0: INTF_0 at MDP + 0x34000, DP1: INTF_4 at + 0x38000), the controller's mainlink, the MSA and TU registers (as `DisplayPort::SetStream` does for DP2), and a second DPU path (SSPP, layer mixer, CTL) on its own frame buffer. Proven on both: the pixel clock at 148.5 MHz (M/N 11/60) runs, and the timing engine with the HDMI output's wide-bus 1080p timing counts 120 frames a second on it, which also confirms the rate (`dp_stream_test()`). A second DPU path for DP0 works too (`dp0_pipeline_test()`): VIG0 with the firmware's VIG2 settings fetching the same frame buffer, LM_0, CTL_0 (CTL_TOP copied, VIG0 in LM_0's blend stage at CTL_LAYER bits 2:0, INTF_0 active, VIG0 fetching, the full flush) and INTF_0: the flush completes, it scans out 60 frames a second on DP0's clock, and neither INTF_0 nor the HDMI output's INTF_6 underruns. The controller's mainlink is written too (`_EnableMainlink()`, then `DisplayPort::SetStream()`/`StartStream()` for controller 0): with no sink it is programmed for 1920x1080 on four lanes at RBR (MSA, M/N, TU) and reports ready for video (MAINLINK_READY 0x401), so the pixels INTF_0 makes go out on the link; whether a monitor shows them needs a monitor.
8. **app_server**: done in `qcom_display` (`usbc_dp.cpp`): connector 0 is output 1 ("DP-1"), its pipe scanning its region of the frame buffer, which app_server's layout sets. Until the link is trained it appears only with the driver setting `usbc_dp0 forced` (settings/kernel/drivers/qcom_display): then `screenmode -d` and the Screen preferences show HDMI-1 and DP-1 (3840x1080), DP-1 runs at 60 Hz (pixel clock 74.25 MHz, M/N 11/120, for the wide-bus timing) without underruns, moving it (`screenmode --display 2 --position 0 1080`) moves its scanout to the matching address, and mirroring (`screenmode --display 2 --mirror 1`, then `off`) has it scan the HDMI output's region and back. Hot plug: `qcom_adsp` keeps each port's last report and exports it (`qcom_usbc_ports.h`, module `drivers/power/qcom_adsp/usbc_ports/v1`); `qcom_display` follows port 0 and connects DP-1 when a DisplayPort sink with HPD is reported, disconnects it when it goes, and tells app_server. Proven with a simulated report (`qcom_adsp` setting `simulate_dp_report 0`: a sink 20 s after the reports are on, gone 30 s later, through the same handler): app_server took DP-1 into a 3840x1080 layout, its path ran without underruns, and went back to 1920x1080 when the sink went. What it waits for: a monitor, to test the training and the picture, and a real report.

Testing steps 6 and 7 against a sink needs a USB-C (or USB-C to DisplayPort/HDMI) monitor to be tested, and the port reports of step 1 to say when to start.
